# Neón al Anochecer

A design philosophy for a city seen from the end of a web at dusk. Written as the art direction for this game's canvas renderer; every visual decision in `Game.ts` answers to it. It documents the look the game already had when it was reworked (the rework changed how the game plays, not how it looks); a new aesthetic should start by rewriting this page with the programmer's direction.

The city is a backdrop, not a set. It is violet on violet, built from flat rectangles in two parallax depths and a third, closer row of towers that carry the antennas. No building has an outline, a texture or a highlight; their only detail is the scatter of warm windows, dim and irregular, that says "people live here" without ever asking for attention. The sky runs from near-black indigo at the top to a bruised rose at the horizon, the minute after sunset — dark enough that anything lit reads instantly.

Pink is the only voice. The pig, the antenna nodes, the score and the titles share one hot pink (`#ff5d8f` and its softer tints), and nothing else in the frame is saturated. That is how the eye finds what matters among a hundred windows: the pig is pink, the things you can grab are pink, and the thing you are about to grab pulses. Gold (`#ffd27f`) is reserved for reward — the "LANZADO" bonus text — and white for the web itself, a single thin line that must be readable against every building.

Information is drawn as light, never as interface. The anchor that a press would grab right now gets a pulsing pale ring and a faint dotted line from the pig; the one you hang from glows brighter. There are no arrows, labels or meters on the play field. When no ring is showing, nothing is in reach — the absence of light is the warning.

The pig is a toy and should look like one: a round body, a radial gradient for volume, a red mask band with web stitching, pointed ears, a snout. It leans into the swing and into the fall, and leaves a short pink ghost trail so speed is visible even when the camera is moving with it. The street is the only true black, with a dashed amber lane line: it is where runs end, so it is the darkest, flattest thing on screen.
